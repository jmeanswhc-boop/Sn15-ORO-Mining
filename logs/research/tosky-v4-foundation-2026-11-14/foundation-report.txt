Recommendation: use the released PIRATE v1 source as the foundation for Tosky_v4.

This is a foundation decision, not approval to submit an unchanged copy and not a
claim that PIRATE has already beaten Tosky_v3 in a controlled test. Keep Tosky_v3
as the budget-accounting and regression reference. Do not merge its whole stack
of controllers into PIRATE. No agent was changed, no Tosky_v4 was created, and
no paid evaluation or submission was run during this analysis.

Public metadata snapshot: 2026-11-14 at 00:06:31 America/New_York
(05:06:31 UTC). Later standings may differ.

The strongest evidence for this choice is PIRATE's better performance on the
same released hidden-race tasks, with measured bills below $5, plus a relatively
coherent implementation of search, requirement interpretation and cart repair.
The additional local result weakens the earlier argument for continuing v3:
its 19/20 result did not repeat, and the full 30-task run exposes failures outside
the negative-review correction that defines v3. Confidence in the engineering
choice is moderate; confidence that it guarantees a future win is not justified.

Detailed failures in the new 30-task run:

a. t-13e82f2c08, cheapest wrap skirt: not the best option, $0.025027, 38 steps.
   The 89-cent selected skirt became unavailable at checkout. At the last three
   steps, the new run removed it and bought a 230-cent replacement. In the earlier
   passing run, the same stockout boundary led to a different replacement and a
   passing order. The repair path did not preserve the cheapest valid choice.
   The trace supports late replacement/optimality failure; it does not justify
   claiming all unseen products were evaluated. Needed component: objective
   preservation during stockout repair, with replacement work reserved earlier.

b. t-86888552db, long-sleeved top: request not met, $0.028371, 37 steps.
   The agent first found and reviewed a fitting-looking top, but checkout reported
   it sold out. It removed the line and spent the remainder searching and reading
   alternatives, then declined. The last audit still proposed an unread candidate
   while reporting no match. The authoritative category says the decline was
   wrong. Needed component: replacement search coverage and a defensible no-match
   decision; the public trace alone does not identify the exact missing accepted
   replacement. This is not a failed API call or an empty-account incident.

c. t-9ab5c575f5, baggy pants or jeans: request not met, $0.023342, 28 steps.
   A checkout notice said the selected item was gone from the shelves. The notice
   parser returned sold_out:true with gone:[] because no identifier was named.
   The next attempt reread attributes and bought exactly the same item. Earlier
   validation replaced its unavailable selection and passed.
   read_note() marks unavailable IDs only when the response names known IDs;
   an unnamed notice sets note_sold but can leave sold_out(id) false. The terminal
   guard checks the latter. Resolving the notice therefore is not equivalent to
   invalidating the affected cart line. Needed component: attach a checkout
   stockout to the attempted cart and invalidate availability/review evidence
   before every terminal path. Do not merely increase the inference allowance.

d. t-9fc67ee841, logo-pattern winter clothing excluding crew neck: request not
   met, $0.025865, 36 steps. Repeated searches for logo/brand-logo and page reads
   ended in a false decline. The explicit constraints were present in the brief.
   The trace indicates search/eligibility coverage, rather than omitted request
   parsing, as the area to inspect. It does not expose a private accepted product.
   Needed component: establish what the search actually covered and discriminate
   an observed mismatch from an unexamined candidate before ending.

e. t-a6d85e34f3, trench coat with alternative closures: needs not found,
   $0.023365, 26 steps. Three preference messages got indifference. In the earlier
   passing run, a bundled question disclosed a girls gender requirement, leading
   to a different choice. In the failing run, a similar broad bundle did not
   disclose it. Both the stock/requirement-change note and final cart check were
   processed, but the missing preference had never entered the ledger. The
   evidence does not support blaming the delivery hint. Needed component:
   interview coverage; a general indifferent reply does not prove every field
   in a long question was resolved.

f. t-abf1c45838, cheapest square clock: did not finish, $0.031256, 37 steps.
   Following a stockout, the repair selected a 67-cent candidate. A late review
   then preferred a 62-cent candidate whose clock_shape field said square while
   its bare shape field said rectangle. The ordinary and repair decisions lacked
   a settled policy for that conflicting evidence, leaving insufficient room
   for a replacement and final action. Local admission denials compounded it.
   Needed components: consistent property scope and cheapest replacement planning.
   A reviewer's claim of a better option is not itself proof of a correct answer.

g. t-eb5b2c8367, monitor: did not finish, $0.016261, 16 steps, 36 model calls.
   The parsed duty was to tell the shopper what to check before committing money.
   The agent sent such a message at step 14. Subsequent procedure reviews returned
   done:false, no evidence and no next action. Checkout was held locally and the
   planner repeatedly requested it without resolving the hold. Additional reads
   changed the evidence set and permitted more review attempts, but no progress.
   The final planner response was token-truncated and subsequent larger requests
   were denied locally despite $0.018739 remaining in the nominal task allowance.
   This is a procedure-interpretation/completion deadlock with ineffective retry
   progress detection. It is NOT evidence that the account exhausted $5. Needed
   components: distinguish informing from obtaining approval, explain unmet
   duties precisely, and stop/replan unchanged failed actions. Do not simply
   remove the gate and allow an unchecked purchase.

The earlier run's sole failure, t-b9f98ac0cc, adds an eighth observed mechanism:
an out-of-stock tunik was replaced by a candidate with a generic blouse type and
a tunik-specific style. Review and planner disagreed over which property defined
the request. The brief had an empty musts ledger, so v3's new typed review path
did not apply and the old adjudicator upheld the negative review. It stopped at
27 world steps, $0.030223, despite 11 steps remaining. The same task passed in
the new run. This supports an interpretation/recovery vulnerability, not a claim
that the product is universally correct or that the task is unsolvable.

The shared problem is maintaining the same live constraints, evidence and
workflow state through search, review, late shopper changes and final action.
A new negative-review decoder cannot fix discovery omissions, stale availability
or a separate procedure deadlock. Conversely, allowing more terminal attempts
does not establish correctness. Any future implementation needs one trustworthy
terminal decision path plus bounded, state-changing repair.

6. What PIRATE actually changed

Closest archived implementation: Giyu v17. All 249 Giyu top-level/method names
occur in PIRATE; 217 have identical AST bodies, 32 are modified, and 56 functions
are added, giving 305 top-level/method definitions excluding nested functions.
136 top-level assignments are identical. HEAR, NOTE and PLAN prompts changed;
16 assignments/constants were added. This is a preserved core with several
coordinated capability additions, not a replacement architecture.

Giyu's source release preceded PIRATE's submission by 38 hours 2 minutes 55
seconds. The structure and timing are consistent with derivation from Giyu or
the same public branch. They do not prove authorship, a direct parent, or how
many development hours were spent. Against Giyu in race 22, PIRATE passed nine
more aggregate episodes at almost identical mean cost ($4.2093 vs $4.2080).
No ablation isolates how much each modification contributed.

The important change groups are:

  Request/quantity interpretation: read_request, quoted_count and name_needs
  recover values embedded in item names and require quantity evidence. This can
  address omissions such as subtype values absent from an explicit musts list.
  The inference is still heuristic; a token in a name is not automatically a
  binding attribute in every context.

  Evidence interpretation: key_verdict, bound_verdict, key_carriers and code_fit
  attach decisions to the matching observed field, handle same-unit numerical
  bounds and reject incompatible references. Unknown is represented separately
  in several branches. Property aliases, conflicting fields and differing units
  remain difficult, and not all shortcuts are safe.

  Discovery: keys_first, interview, settle, read_on, value_pages, noun_search,
  need_search and open_promising change question choice and broaden search before
  a decline. These are relevant to missing preferences and false absence claims.
  PIRATE's own qualifier failures show they do not solve discovery universally.

  Cart/objective repair: price_swap, cap_cart, cheap_swap and short_items repair
  price limits, missing lines and cheaper eligible replacements. They connect
  search to the live cart, rather than only producing another review response.

  Completion: decline_guard, direct_order and finish prevent repeated declines
  or stalled plans from leaving an episode open. Measured completion is strong,
  but some fallback paths bypass the result of the procedure gate.

PIRATE is 6,553 lines / 415,484 bytes versus v3's 11,997 lines / 581,167 bytes.
It has one main Cairn controller, whereas v3's Episode combines 21 direct bases.
Shorter lines and fewer classes do not establish accuracy or scalability by
themselves. The practical advantage is that the next intervention can be reviewed
across fewer interacting override layers. Its single large controller also has
shared mutable state and requires careful tests.

7. Offline-confirmed PIRATE hazards and budget implications

Six isolated method probes were run with synthetic dependencies, no network and
no execution of the archived module's top-level code:

  - direct_order sends buy even when gate('order') returns False.
  - finish likewise sends buy after a negative gate result.
  - gate returns (True, '') if its model response is unavailable.
  - Persistent HTTP 402 causes four physical attempts and 34 nominal seconds of
    waiting before no_credit is latched; the logical call counter increases once.
  - A successful response with missing usage.cost adds zero recorded cost.
  - scaled_same('1000 mAh', '10 V') returns True. This reference-mismatch helper
    compares stripped digits across different units without proving equivalence.

These demonstrate reachable control-flow/helper defects under the stated inputs,
not that every branch fired in a particular public race. In particular, the
procedure-category count cannot be assigned entirely to the forced-checkout bug.

PIRATE's four measured 90-task bills were:
  race 22: $4.120608085 and $4.298073725
  race 23: $4.035243455 and $4.127490265
The most expensive observed run left $0.701926275 below $5. This is encouraging
measured headroom, not a guaranteed future cap. SPEND_SOFT=.055 and
SPEND_HARD=.078 guide model selection; they are not prospective per-request
reservations. Ninety soft allowances already total $4.95; ninety hard allowances
total $7.02. Some calls can bypass the model-selection helper, and the response
is billed before recorded cost changes. The current code cannot certify $5.

V3 is better at financial admission: reservation before each physical request,
conservative accounting for missing costs, immediate 402 stop, and .035 per-task
allocation with .010 protected for procedures within it. Ninety allocations
total $3.15. Its estimator deliberately treats serialized bytes and overhead as
a conservative token envelope; long context can therefore be denied while real
spent dollars remain low. The monitor trace demonstrates why simply retaining
all of v3's admission/repair behavior is not a sufficient solution.

For a future PIRATE-based candidate, preserve the accounting principles but
validate the reservation estimator and compact request shapes separately. A
common $0.05 per-episode target would allocate $4.50 for 90 and $1.50 for 30,
leaving margin under the user's $5/$2 requirements without phase detection.
This is a candidate design bound, not a validated performance setting. It could
cut off expensive useful episodes; no automatic cap change is recommended until
offline cost/admission replay and later authorized evaluation support it. Runtime
pricing, unknown costs and in-flight charges require explicit handling. Do not
derive a hidden-race bill by multiplying the easier practice cost by three.

8. Decision boundary and next development gates

Choose PIRATE as the source tree. Keep v3's successful flows and accounting tests
as comparison material, and keep its originals intact. Continue with v3 only if
a controlled comparison later shows that this exact v3 source retains better
accuracy under the same total budget. Such a head-to-head result is absent now.

When development is authorized, stage the work separately:

  1. Freeze and verify both baseline hashes and the analysis fixtures. Record
     normal-case public tool sequences, terminal outcomes, paid requests and
     costs before changing any shopping logic.

  2. Implement one central inference admission/accounting path in the PIRATE
     candidate. Count physical retries, stop immediately on 402, retain uncertain
     reservations, and keep sufficient completion resources. Test boundary,
     missing-cost and retry cases offline. Healthy affordable calls must preserve
     their models, request content and normal sequence.

  3. Fix one coherent terminal/procedure contract: all checkout and decline paths
     must honor an unresolved duty or changed cart, and failed reviews must yield
     an actionable repair or a bounded stop. Include a synthetic analogue of
     v3's monitor deadlock so the fix cannot merely exchange false permission
     for repeated noncompletion. Preserve completed duties when their relevant
     state has not changed. Do not transplant the whole v3 procedure class.

  4. Independently quarantine unproven numeric-equivalence decisions and verify
     stockout/cheapest-replacement cases, using synthetic products and runtime
     schemas. Keep this separate from the budget and procedure changes so that
     any regression has an identifiable cause. Do not import answer tables.

  5. Run local security/AST checks and evaluate meaningful reachable originality.
     ORO permits building on released code, but cosmetic forks may be rejected.
     An archived champion's acceptance does not preapprove a new submission.
     Only after offline gates pass should separately authorized, bounded paid
     comparison of the exact originals and candidate establish score, cost and
     timing. No paid test was run or promised as part of this selection analysis.

The foundation recommendation does not claim that changing one gate guarantees
a championship or zero regressions. It identifies the stronger observed starting
point and the specific risks that would otherwise repeat the earlier failures.
