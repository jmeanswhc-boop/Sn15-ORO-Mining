TOSKY competitive architecture review — 2026-11-13, America/New_York

Decision

Continue developing the new TOSKY architecture, but do not treat the current
file as ready for a championship attempt. Its advantage is the opportunity to
make the decision process small, explicit and testable. Its present evidence,
state and continuation contracts still reproduce several failure mechanisms
that harmed the older agents. It also lacks an enforceable inference-dollar
budget. Neither superior accuracy nor a safe full-run bill has been measured.

The central accuracy issue is the transition from model interpretation to
committed state and the next action. The controller sometimes accepts a claim
because a quotation exists without proving the relevant product/property;
elsewhere it rejects a formatting mistake as an enduring unknown and never
repairs it. Required information can be scheduled after the decision needing it,
and completed work can invalidate itself. A reliable lifecycle for claims and
their dependencies addresses this group of weaknesses together. Interviewing,
ranking, transport and financial admission also need separate acceptance checks.

No production agent was changed. No paid inference, evaluation or submission was
performed. The source remains agents/TOSKY.py, 82,547 bytes, SHA-256:
31b083e8ac95bc6863a4a01f9768a61879914fed4eb9116ea0f27b394d825a18

1. What evidence supports this assessment

The review uses the prior champion-lineage study, Chidori budget/recovery
incidents, the v4 development review, the complete public race-22 comparison,
the Tosky_v2 completion-collapse investigation, the Tosky_v3 recovery analysis,
and the earlier function-by-function TOSKY audit. Thirteen prior report files
are indexed by hash in review-evidence.json. The archived champion study covers
38 unique title versions and 41 title events before the later Pooh title; it
distinguishes titleholders from race-only winners. The current source inventory
contains 43 archive files, all parsed here without executing third-party agents.
Focused source review compares KYO, Cairn, Giyu, CHIDORI and Pooh mechanisms.

I reread all 30 suite-160 public qualifying goals, joined their recorded results,
and built an individual review row for each. All 720 available current comparison
feedback records were loaded and their outcomes recounted: 180 qualifying plus
540 race-22 executions. This is two runs per agent on 30 and 90 distinct tasks,
respectively, not 60 and 180 independent task designs. The earlier 72 targeted
nonchampion qualifier records and their six-agent comparison were also used.

Evidence levels are kept distinct:
- Public goals and released observations establish requirements and observed
  behavior. A score category reports at most one failure, not every cause.
- Controlled controller probes establish what code accepts or refuses under a
  specified input. They do not establish how frequently an LLM produces it.
- Static review identifies missing mechanisms and possible new-world failures.
- Hidden future task contents, private grading logic and future scores remain
  unknown. None were reconstructed or inserted into production code.

Provider, validator and world variation mean these comparisons are descriptive,

The earlier v4 used less than half of Pooh's mean race-22 cost and only two fewer

Twenty v4 executions stopped at the agent's choice; the public records did not
expose which local guard, admission decision or exception caused each stop.

The old incidents show several distinct causes that must not be collapsed into
one budget explanation:
- Chidori v1's mandatory pricing GET failed authentication before any shopping.
  The mock client had hidden the transport defect during prior tests.
- V2's real inference requests succeeded, but change handling and restrictive
  local admission impaired useful decisions.
- V3 detected more invalid states but could not reliably recover from them.
- Tosky_v2's new review boundary led to 0/30 and 2/30 public results. In the
  supplied ten-case local run, all 322 model requests succeeded, yet every task
  stopped. Rejected representation, mismatched evidence contracts and wrong
  repair routing were major mechanisms; an API outage was not the common cause.
- Tosky_v3's offline recovery checks do not establish a score for this new file.

The champion history also rejects code-volume rules. Giyu's changes relative to
Cairn gained about 3.33 percentage points in race 18, lost 0.56 in race 19, gained
3.90 in race 20 and lost 0.56 in race 21; validator overlap differs. The earlier
PricePilot audit found added helpers unreachable from the retained parent flow.
Neither code growth nor time-to-title proves a useful functional improvement.

3. Advantages and disadvantages against the champion designs

KYO already combined model interpretation with a deterministic ledger, question
planning, reference handling and finishing controls. Cairn extended dynamic
record shapes, pagination, references, multi-item behavior and recovery. Giyu
added narrow numeric/property/search interventions. CHIDORI elaborated evidence,
compatibility and interview handling. Pooh adds adaptive question width and a
literal four-state evidence model among many other mechanisms. Explicit state,
evidence checking and multiple model roles are therefore not novel by themselves.

TOSKY's useful advantages are concrete but mostly engineering opportunities:

A. A much smaller controller to inspect.
The source is approximately 82.5 kB versus KYO's 170.3 kB, Cairn's 325.7 kB,
CHIDORI's 480.2 kB and Pooh's 774.2 kB. Four dataclasses and one controller expose
item, candidate, scope and obligation state. This makes invariants and focused
changes easier to review. Small source does not prove low token cost or accuracy.

B. A common place to control model spending.
All ten semantic call sites pass through infer. A single admission mechanism can
cover selected-model calls and backups. At present this is only an opportunity:
the code records cost after the fact and does not enforce dollars.

C. Explicit basket and procedure state.
The heap-based joint search gives a place to handle several items under shared
constraints. checkout_ready checks normal-path obligations. In the inspected
Pooh forced-finish path, gate is called but its returned hold is not honored
before act. TOSKY has a clearer central gate to strengthen. Its own scheduling
and fallback defects mean it has not yet solved the same problem end to end.

D. Correct protocol foundations.
The controller copies the complete binding, numbers steps itself, retries an
uncertain environment request with the same payload and blocks further world
actions after finished. The positive offline controls support these properties.
These are necessary foundations, not unique competitive advantages.

E. Task-derived state instead of persistent answer storage.
Runtime caches are recreated per task. Tools are discovered from supplied
schemas. This suits generated worlds and permits compliance without storing
qualifier answers or fixed product-property tables.

Capabilities where the champions currently have more developed machinery:
- Adaptive interviewing and use of remaining questions; TOSKY stops optional
  questions after two and marks clarification replies as answered.
- Property/numeric matching: Pooh's literal_state checks property-named keys,
  numerical limits, alternatives and missing/differing/unknown states. TOSKY's
  matches protocol mostly trusts the semantic true/false supplied by the model.
- Reference/search recovery and alternative-query handling; TOSKY can cache an
  unknown as current and have no next action capable of resolving it.
- Operational wind-down and bounded late recovery. TOSKY has per-request
  timeouts but lacks a whole-episode budget-aware completion policy.

Those mechanisms should inform isolated tests. Copying their complete code or
adding every feature would reintroduce coupling and untested changes.

4. Public qualifier findings, with concrete tests

All 30 public goals allow three messages and 38 world steps. Nine explicitly
require the cheapest eligible option; the other 21 do not acquire that objective
merely because cheaper products exist. The complete per-task mapping, original
goals, old outcomes and relevant code risks are in qualifier-matrix.txt and
review-evidence.json. These are research fixtures, not production routing data.

The six previously troublesome tasks provide especially useful controls:

Dining set with bench seating:
CHIDORI, old Tosky and Pooh each failed both recorded qualifier executions.
Four Leaf and PRIMO each passed both. In one PRIMO trace the first two messages
receive indifference; the third discovers the table-base preference. TOSKY's
question_to_ask at 1061-1070 rejects an optional third question even with 37 world
steps left and a message allowance of three. The new offline probe confirms this
without assuming a wrong model answer. A third useful question must be possible;
it must not be implemented as a fixed dining-category question table.

Single-pack cola:
All three baselines failed both; all six selected nonchampions passed both.
Pack count, cart quantity, packaging and pack type must remain separate. The new
probe supplies a wrong model judgment citing pack_size=1 as proof of pack type.
TOSKY accepts it because the quotation is real. A correct negative model judgment
is honored, and a correct pack-type positive control passes. This proves a weak
validation boundary, not an inevitable model error on this qualifier.

Prescription glasses:
All three baselines failed both; all six selected nonchampions passed both.
The public request concerns capability reaching +/-6 and a lens-color alternative.
The passing comparison behavior supports a decline when the available products
cannot establish the requested capability. A narrow range is not coverage of a
larger range, and frame color is not lens color. New probes show that TOSKY accepts
incorrect true judgments with real quotations from a narrow range or the wrong
component. Correct false judgments are preserved. Its quote checking does not
independently validate the relationship, operator or component.

Sugar-free cola:
Old Tosky passed neither, CHIDORI one and Pooh both. Five of six selected
nonchampions passed both; the sixth passed one. The interview must discover the
unsettled preference, use the property actually named in the reply and retain
indifference as settled state. TOSKY's two-question cutoff, free-field omission
and asked-before-understanding behavior can repeat the old discovery problem.

Polyester sweater:
Old Tosky and Pooh each passed one; CHIDORI and all six sampled nonchampions
passed both. Earlier traces show a preference reply turned into extraneous search
wording, followed by a false decline despite seeing the eventual passing item.
TOSKY's exact free-text requirement strings, incomplete delta schema and absent
format-only recovery do not establish that this interpretation problem is fixed.

Slip-on winter footwear:
Old Tosky and CHIDORI each passed one; Pooh and all six sampled nonchampions
passed both. Product category, closure exclusions and the shopper's disclosed
property all matter. Search similarity cannot supply missing item-type proof.
TOSKY passes type/role to the model but requires no independently checked type
result; empty hard requirements can make eligibility vacuously true.

The other 24 tasks are preservation controls, not expendable easy examples.
They include exact voltage, dietary requirements, pure composition, negative
material/color/sleeve requirements, minimum screen size, alternative categories,
component-specific properties and indifference. The new negation probe shows
that a wrong semantic true judgment with a literal leather quotation is accepted
for a not-leather requirement. A repair must preserve real negation, OR choices,
correct inequality direction and product/component ownership across all cases.

The new probes comprise ten scenarios: one third-message counterexample, four
semantic-validation counterexamples, four corresponding correct-negative
controls and one correct-positive control. They make no live-model accuracy
claim. The prior 34 counterexamples and nine positive controls still refer to
the identical source hash; they are not 34 failed benchmark tasks.

5. Hidden-race risks: what is demonstrated and what is inferred

ORO generates separate race worlds. Released feedback does not reveal the original
private request or all grading checks. The following are robustness requirements,
not a prediction of a future task list.

Evidence ownership and freshness — demonstrated code defects:
- A first comparison response can retain the whole response as a candidate's
  detail; another product's fact can then pass its quote check.
- Old stock can be promoted to the latest offer step despite a newer stockout.
- Literal dotted keys cannot be addressed unambiguously by split-on-dot paths.
- A malformed result becomes an evaluated/current unknown; later observations
  or references do not always invalidate it, and next_unverified skips it.
These risks become more consequential with heterogeneous records and changes.

Atomic request changes — demonstrated code defects:
- Adds are applied before drops. Replacing a property can erase the newly added
  value along with the old one.
- Drops lack reliable source-quote validation. Budget units, quantities and free
  states are incomplete, and new procedural requirements have no proper delta.
The design needs a transaction that preserves exactly the latest active request.

References and procedures — demonstrated control-flow defects:
- A reference needed to prove eligibility can be read only once a basket exists,
  although that basket requires the same eligibility: a circular dependency.
- A required read can be marked complete by a nonempty message response.
- A before-selection duty can fail and the controller can still align the cart.
- A successful read changes offer metadata and invalidates its own basket key.
The last case is particularly expensive even with faithful scripted replies.

With the public 38-step allowance, the four complete controlled flows produced:
ordinary purchase: 7 world steps, 17 model requests, correct scripted order;
format error: 6 steps, 14 requests, decline without repairing the candidate;
reference dependency: 6 steps, 14 requests, decline without reading the reference;
required read: 26 steps, 90 requests, 20 evaluations, decline after repeated reads.
The required-read case used 60 Sonnet calls and 30 Haiku calls. Increasing its
allowance from the earlier 20-step fixture raised request consumption from 54
to 90 without curing the cycle. This is a logic failure, not measured API latency
or actual race cost, and it does not require a bad semantic judgment.

Cart and optimization — demonstrated/static defects:
- A quantity can be accepted by matching an unrelated number, such as price.
- A projected cart can omit an observed extra line and still be called exact.
- checkout_ready can be true while a known cheaper candidate is unresolved.
- Physical objective units are not normalized: the existing probe ranks 900 g
  above 2 kg for a maximum objective.
- The 600-combination cutoff and unknown compatibility can return the same empty
  result as genuine infeasibility. Reusing one listing for several requested
  quantities is excluded/overwritten rather than correctly aggregated.

World-schema variation — static risks:
valid_schema omits string-length/pattern constraints. Generic required procedure
tools that do not map to the fixed role menu can be excluded from do_process.
Success:false without an error key is not consistently treated as a failed
action. An ordinary numeric word such as within can be mistaken for a reference.
Long records are clipped by character counts, sometimes losing the evidence the
validator expects. These all require generated-schema variation tests.

Generalization tests must vary properties, wording, identifiers, record nesting,
units, pagination, tool names, procedure ordering and changes. The invariant is
the relationship between evidence and a decision. No test should depend on
memorized public answers, a fixed hidden-family label or a private grader.

6. Both monetary limits must constrain the design

Current documented caps are $2 per qualifying run and $5 per race run. For the
30/90 task counts used here, equal shares are $0.06667 and $0.05556 per task.
The race permits 16.7% less spending per task while having harder unseen cases.
For example, repeating a $1.90 qualifier workload three times would cost $5.70.
The qualifier limit therefore cannot be used as the race's affordability test.

TOSKY has no dollar admission control. self.budget is the shopper's product budget;
reserve() reserves world actions. model_cost is retrospective bookkeeping. The
38-step public tasks permit up to 90 physical model attempts per task. Missing
usage and uncertain charges are not conservatively reserved; a 402 does not stop
backup/later inference. A terminal receipt still triggers interpretation.

The November 13 archived public pricing snapshot gives Sonnet $2-$2.20 input and
$10-$11 output per million tokens. Its initial 3,300 output-token allowance permits
$0.033-$0.0363 output cost alone. Haiku base-tier rates are $0.10-$0.11 input and
$0.50-$0.55 output; the catalog has a higher tier at 100,000 prompt tokens.
DeepSeek is the backup. SANDBOX_MODEL can change both normal and deep routes.
These dated snapshots are not a guarantee of a future route price.

Full-output-allowance arithmetic, excluding input, for the controlled successful
path is $0.0987-$0.10857 per task. The 38-step procedure-loop path permits
$0.7125-$0.78375 of output. These are stress envelopes, not actual expected bills;
short replies cost less, and inputs/other billing effects require reconciliation.

Uniform per-task planning envelopes, not current settings or validated guarantees:
USD/task     30-task allocation/headroom     90-task allocation/headroom
0.035        1.05 / 0.95                    3.15 / 1.85
0.040        1.20 / 0.80                    3.60 / 1.40
0.045        1.35 / 0.65                    4.05 / 0.95

One uniform policy can respect both phases without prohibited phase routing.
A preliminary operating target near $3.60 per 90 tasks leaves room for uncertainty,
but $0.040 per task is not yet a proven feasible allocation. Simply wrapping the
current 3,300-token deep initialization with that limit could starve the rest of
the episode. Allocations must fund a complete correct path, including needed
questions, recovery and terminal actions.

The design requirements are prospective reservation for every physical attempt,
actual-model/tier accounting, conservative settlement of missing/uncertain costs,
bounded backup attempts, proper credit-stop handling and protected resources for
completion. Remove work after finished and repeated unchanged interpretations.
Cache validated runtime schema/fact bindings; reserve expensive semantic calls for
ambiguity that affects the decision. Every model/prompt/output change needs its
own useful-output and correctness checks. A smaller output cap that produces
truncated JSON can increase total cost and reduce accuracy.

The agent should use per-task RAM state, not a shared file-backed money ledger or
direct provider-key management. Run-wide provider enforcement stops new requests;
in-flight work can still finish. Conservative reservations and headroom are needed
in addition to that cap. Account-level totals across independent validator runs
are a separate matter from one $5 run.

Time is also a resource: 45-second inference requests and three 70-second world
attempts can consume the documented default five-minute task allowance. The
controller must have an affordable bounded continuation/finish policy; a request
count alone does not establish compliance with task or run time limits.

7. Submission requirements and storage review

The unchanged source passes local UTF-8, Python AST and compilation checks, has
a synchronous agent_main and is under 1 MB. Its direct calls use the supplied
ProxyClient and environment/inference routes. No direct network/file-writing,
shell/native/dynamic execution, prohibited encoding import, fixed answer table,
grader import, scoped-key manipulation or phase/validator routing was found.
The supported SANDBOX_MODEL override and per-task RAM caches are consistent with
the public contract. The earlier live allowlist snapshot contained all three
coded model IDs; this turn's attempted refresh was blocked.

Supplied ProxyClient instrumentation may append harness logs. This is distinct
from agent-created persistent storage; TOSKY does not choose those destinations
or read logs as shopping memory. It requests no provider-native browsing/bash
tools. Peak production RAM remains unmeasured, and observations are retained.

All improvements must stay generic: runtime-derived property/requirement/evidence
identifiers are acceptable design inputs, while product IDs, fixed attribute/unit
registries, private grading taxonomy and memorized qualifier answers are not to
be embedded. The same strategy must serve qualifying and race. Official server
security, integrity, similarity and analysis-time admission remain untested;
local parsing cannot certify them. Historical acceptance of a champion is not
a blanket exemption for a newly submitted derivative.

8. Concrete review-to-development sequence

Stage 0 — Freeze the baseline and the evidence contract.
Preserve this source hash, all 30 qualifier rows, recorded passing controls and
counterexamples. Give each active requirement an item, property/component,
operator, alternatives and source span. Give each observation fact a runtime ID,
entity, exact field path, typed value/unit, source tool and observation version.
Keep MET, UNMET, UNKNOWN and INVALID_REPRESENTATION distinct. Do not demand one
global completeness boolean that turns every missing field into shopping failure.
Acceptance: legitimate OR/negative/range/indifference states can be represented;
no omitted explicit requirement silently becomes a valid empty contract.

Stage 1 — Feasible cost admission and observability, separately verified.
Use infer as the single admission point. Test with the actual ProxyClient
transport boundary, not just a fake high-level client. Show complete successful
paths fit the chosen allocation before imposing it. Add compact returned
diagnostics identifying call purpose, model, reservation, reported/uncertain cost,
finish reason and exact validation error. No custom output files.
Acceptance: missing-cost, timeout, fallback, 402, model override and 30/90-task
accounting workloads remain bounded; a valid finishing path remains reachable.

Stage 2 — Evidence binding and bounded representation repair.
Update evaluate and observation bindings to validate the requested type, entity,
component, field and operator. Use exact path segments or runtime evidence IDs.
Repair malformed representation using existing facts and a precise rejected
field; do not reread a product to fix JSON. Genuine negative facts stay negative.
Acceptance: reject the semantic counterexamples without rejecting the positive
controls; a format-only failure can recover and finish the healthy full flow.

Stage 3 — Dependency and state lifecycle.
Apply request revisions atomically. Invalidate only conclusions affected by a
material change. Separate one-time reads, product eligibility, cart-dependent
approval and deadlines. Schedule a reference as soon as a required claim depends
on it, even before a basket exists. A new hold must expose an executable next
action and a bounded no-progress policy.
Acceptance: the reference and repeated-read flows reach the intended valid
order; unchanged reads do not reopen duties; changed stock/requirements revoke
only dependent conclusions; early duties are performed before the relevant act.

Stage 4 — Interview policy as an isolated change.
Allow a useful third question when the public budget permits it; reserve all
necessary message duties for the relevant ending branch. Separate asked,
answered, indifferent, clarified and unresolved states. Derive questions from
the request, observed differences and replies, not a fixed category dictionary.
Acceptance: reproduce the third-message opportunity and preserve earlier
successful disclosures; do not infer live response quality from scripted replies.

Stage 5 — Exact cart, objective and tool behavior.
Bind quantities to their actual fields and check all observed lines. Resolve
known decision-relevant cheaper alternatives on cheapest tasks; keep any tasks
free of an invented cheapest-proof duty. Normalize units from task-time evidence,
retain explicit unknown coverage/cutoff state, and validate supplied schemas.
Acceptance: extra-line, quantity, unit, same-listing, pagination, unknown-tool and
acknowledgement cases pass alongside unchanged ordinary purchase/decline paths.

Each substage needs a separate source version/checkpoint and a before/after
control-flow comparison. Do not combine prompt, model, interviewer and evidence
changes and then try to infer their contribution from one overall score.

Only after these gates should separately authorized paid evaluation compare the
actual baseline and candidate on matching public worlds. Measure paired wins AND
losses, correct endings, true cost per correct task, all billable attempts and
latency tails. A recorded response cannot be reused as the answer to a changed
prompt or model. Public-qualifier coverage must then be complemented by generic
unseen-schema/state-change tests; a higher qualifier score alone cannot certify
hidden-race strength or an eventual championship title.
