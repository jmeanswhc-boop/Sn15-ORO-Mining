Tosky_v4 review of the supplied 20-task and 30-task verification runs
2026-11-14

Tosky_v4 has not demonstrated an accuracy improvement. The full run is 21/30
(70%), compared with Tosky_v3's 23/30 on the same practice pack. Its mean task
time is substantially lower and its inference cost is slightly lower. The
remaining problems concern preserving requirements, discovering preferences,
interpreting product properties consistently, and retaining good candidates
during recovery. They are not explained by an OpenRouter credit outage.

The additional full run changes the priority of the diagnosis: the narrow
interview is a confirmed new weakness, but loss of explicit requirements is
also a substantial, separate failure cluster. A broad rewrite or simply
increasing the dollar allowance would not address either mechanism reliably.

This is analysis only. No agent, previous version, test suite, deployment or
submission was changed. No paid evaluation, inference call or external message
was made. The new files in this directory are research artifacts. Existing
offline loader utilities blocked unscripted transport during diagnostic probes.

The identical 70% scores conceal different outcomes. On the 20 shared tasks:
  - 12 pass in both A and B.

Detailed failure evidence

1. Wedding mules — t-559c052a99, fails in A and B.

The original request says the mules are for a wedding. Both v4 brief responses
contain an empty musts list. The shopper then successfully discloses pattern:
animal print. Both runs buy p-904371f2b08b for 595 cents: its pattern matches,
but its own occasion is casual. The internal cart review returns OK.

Both older v3 runs preserve occasion: wedding and pass with p-a556ba22bad6 at
605 cents. That candidate was also present in v4 A's review context. V4's
cheaper choice is not a valid saving if it drops the requested use/occasion.
An offline probe of the existing key checker rejects the chosen casual product
when occasion: wedding is supplied. The omission is therefore directly
actionable; the checker already has the machinery to reject this contradiction.

The root defect is loss of an explicit requirement during request extraction,
followed by a review that fails to reconcile its simplified ledger with the raw
request. The CHECK prompt does include the raw request; merely including it
again is demonstrably insufficient. A Sonnet review was locally denied in each
run, but the missing requirement exists before that denial. A more expensive
review might react differently; that outcome has not been measured.

2. Youth-fashion hoodie — t-70ef000b3f, fails in A and B.

In A the questions are gender, pattern, hoodie style. In B they are gender,
hoodie style, season. Every reply is indifferent. Both older v3 runs ask about
material within their question coverage, elicit nylon, and successfully decline
after searching. V4 instead buys a product with the requested youth-fashion
style, without discovering the material requirement.

The new ranker is directly implicated in B: the next proposed question was
material, and question_focus changes it to season. Replaying the actual read
catalog and recorded plan reproduces that override offline. There is no local
budget denial in either v4 hoodie execution.

The recorded failure category is request not met, not needs not found. The
documented category includes ordering when a decline was required. The prior
successful decline after discovering nylon strongly supports an interview and
feasibility-decision failure; the public verdict does not expose the individual
predicate needed to prove that entire counterfactual. The visible youth-fashion
property itself is not missing from the chosen products.

3. Luxury/formal watch — t-82a9890683, fails in A and B.

A asks color, options and dial glass. B asks strap, dial glass and options.
Neither asks case shape or gender. Prior successful v3 traces elicited case
shape: rectangle; one also elicited gender: female. The current verdict is
needs not found in both runs. Color-family relaxation and stockout recovery
occur, but neither substitutes for discovering the missing preference.

Offline reproduction with A's six read records gives:
  options:    coverage 5/6, value diversity 0.80, product 0.6667
  dial glass: coverage 5/6, value diversity 0.56, product 0.4667
  gender:     coverage 5/6, value diversity 0.48, product 0.4000
  case shape: coverage 4/6, value diversity 0.375, product 0.2500

The ranker selects options. Existing NOT_PROPERTY already identifies options
as metadata, but focus_question does not apply that eligibility rule. Earlier
lane construction puts metadata behind meaningful properties; the new ranker
can promote it again. The formula measures variation among products, not the
probability that a shopper has an undisclosed requirement about that property.
This is a reproduced implementation flaw, not just an unfavorable model answer.

4. Trench coat — t-a6d85e34f3, fails in A and B.

A asks pattern, collar type and length. B asks material, collar and jacket coat
styles. Neither asks gender. An earlier passing v3 trace elicited gender: girls.
The intervening v3 full run also missed that preference and failed. Both v4
verdicts are needs not found. A ends after 34 steps; B ends after 17, leaving
considerable step/time room but no messages. This is an irreversible interview
budget failure, not an exhausted inference-dollar budget.

The new single-property policy reduces coverage, and the diversity rule favors
other catalog fields. However, this weakness also existed in v3: broad lists
were not uniformly reliable either. Restoring every old question verbatim is
not a demonstrated complete solution.

5. Logo-pattern winter clothing — t-9fc67ee841, passes A, fails B.

B's parsed hard requirements contain only collar: not crew neck. The requested
logo pattern disappears entirely from musts, wishes and rules. B buys
p-00e4e97823f5 at 496 cents, whose collar is hooded and pattern is solid color.
Its internal review returns OK. An offline probe rejects the same product when
pattern: logo is included. A passed by choosing a different product. The new B
failure is a concrete instance of the same extraction/coverage defect as the
wedding request; it is not evidence that the collar exclusion should be relaxed.

6. Baggy pants OR jeans — t-9ab5c575f5, fails B; not sampled in A.

The request explicitly accepts pants or jeans. The raw brief reduces that to
name: pants. At about 31.6 seconds, line_hold rejects p-028af8354258 solely
because its category is Jeans. Its price is 173 cents, below the 395 cap, and
its read fit attributes say baggy fit. Offline replay confirms owner_of('Jeans')
returns 0 for the narrowed item while key_verdict(fit: baggy) is true.

The agent continues searching, finally carts a 354-cent candidate at step 36,
and first attempts checkout at step 37. That candidate sells out. The agent
identifies a 355-cent alternative but has only one move left and declines at
step 38. An earlier audit also names the 173-cent Jeans candidate as fitting.

There are two linked defects: dropping an explicit category alternative and
spending the action budget before attempting a recoverable purchase. The new
stockout handling recognizes the sold-out line; it cannot recover steps already
spent rejecting permitted alternatives and searching. The requested OR must be
preserved, not repaired with a task-specific list of product IDs.

7. Tunik — t-b9f98ac0cc, fails B; not sampled in A.

The request specifies top type: tunik. The brief turns tunik into the item name
and leaves musts empty. Consequently the cart ledger literally says there are
no requirements beyond the kind of product. After a stockout, replacement
selection accepts p-02d14c7e9b88 at 637 cents because mus_top_styles and
muslim_top_style say tunik. It does not establish the requested top-type field.
The checker returns OK and the final verdict is request not met.

The earlier v3 full run also had an empty musts list but happened to pass with
a different replacement, p-2610c21f6e2a, whose top.type is tunic. An older v3
run failed this task. That contrast supports a type-versus-style evidence
problem; it does not prove every related catalog key is always invalid. The
confirmed defect is the lost typed predicate and lack of a consistent proof
requirement across selection, replacement and review.

8. Cheapest square clock — t-abf1c45838, passes A, fails B.

B reads and reviews a 62-cent candidate, p-180b10b52cb0. Its own review names
that candidate among cheaper fitting clocks. It first tries a 53-cent clock,
which sells out at step 33, then replaces it with a 67-cent clock and orders at
step 37. The result is not the best option.

The replacement prompt omitted the known 62-cent product. The code takes only
the newest 25 seen records. Offline reconstruction puts the 62-cent candidate
42nd in that ordering, so it is excluded. The extra cheapest-row recovery then
only considers unopened rows; this candidate was already opened, so that path
cannot restore it. Sorting the model's returned fits by observed price cannot
recover a candidate the model was never shown.

At the final checkout only two actions remain. run_calls' substantial review
block is conditional on left() > 2, so the replacement is not given another
recorded CHECK before ordering. The defect is not lack of price information;
it is loss of an already-known candidate plus insufficient recovery/review room.
A passed with the 62-cent product, but only after conflicting checks and 37
steps. Its pass did not mean the underlying selection logic was repaired.

9. Long-sleeved top — t-86888552db, fails B; not sampled in A.

The brief retains sleeve length: long. A line classifier first rejects a
Fall/Winter Clothing candidate, then the agent selects a Tops candidate. After
that item sells out, it orders p-077e3cbd7819 for 605 cents. The final observed
category is Tops, stock is true, quantity is one, and sleeve.length is long
sleeve. The internal review returns OK; the evaluator returns request not met.

There is an observable category-model disagreement earlier in the trace, but
the final public record appears to meet the stated category, sleeve, budget and
stock constraints. Its specific failing evaluator predicate is not exposed.
It would be unjustified to claim the final product has short sleeves, blame a
credit failure, or assert a scoring bug. This case needs a focused trace-based
investigation of category/property semantics and any undiscovered feasibility
condition before a targeted patch can be justified. It is not diagnosed to the
same confidence as the mules, logo, category-alternative or clock defects.

Two additional cases qualify the conclusions

The wrap skirt (t-13e82f2c08) fails A and passes B at the final allowed step.
The known 89-cent product has generic style: casual and skirt style: wrap
skirts. key_verdict('style: wrap') rejects it because an exact generic key ranks
above an item-scoped key. The CHECK and SWAP prompts instead explicitly allow
an item-scoped property to establish the value. The same input therefore gets
opposite answers from different components. Offline probes reproduce false for
style: wrap and true for skirt style: wrap. A cycles between 89- and 98-cent
products, then falsely declines after the 98-cent one sells out. B first loses
the 89-cent one to stockout and ends successfully with the 98-cent alternative.
The changed outcome does not remove the internal semantic contradiction.

The cleanser (t-b9e9292a88) fails A and passes B. A's final product is 139 cents,
in stock, in Facial Cleanser, and its visible skin benefit literally says acne
care; the internal checker agrees. B passes with a different, anti-pimple
cleanser, even though the code's literal key checker flags that value as not
matching acne care. This demonstrates code/semantic-review inconsistency, but
does not establish which undisclosed predicate caused A's failure. It would be
incorrect to claim that asking skin type is proven to fix A: neither B nor the
older passing v3 full run elicited a new skin-type preference. The opaque verdict
supports a failure, not a precise explanation for every matching-looking record.

Common structure of the defects

The controller does not enforce one complete, consistent task contract through
the whole sequence of decisions. The relevant stages are:
  original request -> parsed items/musts -> questions -> candidate eligibility
  -> stockout replacement -> internal review -> terminal action.

At different stages it can:
  - lose an explicit predicate or one side of an OR;
  - spend all interview messages without discovering consequential preferences;
  - treat generic and item-scoped keys differently in code and model prompts;
  - forget a previously validated cheaper candidate when packing a new prompt;
  - reach a terminal deadline with too little room for recovery or rechecking.

These are related evidence-consistency failures, but they do not all have a
single safe one-line fix. A new architecture is not justified by these logs.
The useful narrow work is to repair each broken handoff and test it separately.

The request-preservation cluster now deserves first attention: mules and logo
are direct omitted predicates, pants is an explicit lost alternative, and
tunik loses its typed predicate. Interview selection is the next separate
priority, with three strongly supported affected tasks. Those counts identify
exposed failure paths; they are not a promise of recovering seven scores.

Budget, cost allocation and runtime

There was no $5 credit outage. A has 9 local Sonnet denials across 5 tasks;
B has 10 across 7 tasks. Three of those A tasks and five B tasks still pass.
Four of A's six failures and seven of B's nine have no local denial. No charge
is missing; no request is logged as failing; no reservation breach is recorded.
B's most expensive task costs $0.03795042, below the configured $0.050 allowance.

These are local admission denials, distinct from provider HTTP failures. The
guard intentionally counts serialized input bytes as an upper estimate of
tokens, includes framing overhead and adds a 25% margin. In A the median input
estimate is about 3.01 times the actual prompt-token count. In the mules case,
Sonnet is denied at $0.01885 spent despite $0.03115 nominal allowance remaining;
the corresponding fallback prompt has 4,402 actual input tokens versus a
12,376-token admission estimate. The fallback still executes successfully.
This is conservative model routing, not evidence that the account ran dry.

The more important allocation issue is:
  A question planning: 20 Sonnet calls, $0.221422, 52.30% of total spend.
  B question planning: 30 Sonnet calls, $0.329148, 53.11% of total spend.
  A cart review: 36 calls, $0.0223409, 5.28% of total spend.
  B cart review: 49 calls, $0.0334155, 5.39% of total spend.
Only one of A's and two of B's successful cart-review calls use Sonnet. The
agent pays for a strong question planner, then its local diversity heuristic
overrides parts of that plan, sometimes to ask metadata or already-covered
properties. More expensive planning therefore does not guarantee better
preference discovery.

Among A's 59 preference questions (the 60th shopper message is an explanation),
the HEAR parser extracts just two new requirements, in two tasks. B's 89
preference questions extract three in three tasks. Negative answers can be
useful, so these counts alone are not an accuracy metric. Together with the
prior disclosed hoodie/watch/coat requirements, they show why spending more on
the same question policy is unlikely to be the first productive intervention.

At this practice-task average, simple scaling gives about $1.86-$1.91 for 90
executions. That is arithmetic under an identical task mix, not a hidden-race
cost forecast or guarantee. The configured $0.050 per invocation allocates
$4.50 across 90, or $1.50 across 30. Its safeguards depend on the configured
rate envelope, billing fidelity and number of executions; reruns and unexpected
provider prices are not made free. The present evidence does not justify
raising the overall cap. First reduce repeated prompts, preserve good
candidates, and reserve concise review capacity within the existing envelope.

There is one smaller observed efficiency issue: in A the monitor procedure
receives five gate reviews, costing $0.0041519 and 25.04 seconds of model time.
After stockout, two of those reviews spend $0.0018945 and 11.46 seconds verifying
the same earlier explanation again. procedure_key includes the global version,
so a stock change can invalidate a non-cart-dependent explanation. B uses three
gate reviews and passes too. Dependency-aware reuse is a later optimization,
not a reason to weaken proof requirements or combine another speculative change
with the accuracy fixes.

What should be preserved

The monitor task passes in both v4 runs. Its third message is actually reserved
for the requested explanation, and the gate eventually recognizes the recorded
proof. That is stronger evidence than the earlier scripted fixture alone,
although one task does not establish all hidden procedure behavior.

The new unnamed-stockout scope logic activates eight times in A (six of those
tasks pass) and fourteen times in B. In the examined failures it recognizes the
unavailable cart line; downstream selection or action-budget problems remain.
Retain that scoping, the prevention of stale/unavailable terminal orders, the
evidence-sensitive review cache, physical-call accounting, missing-charge
reservation and immediate credit-error stop. A score failure does not imply
every component involved should be reverted.

The public checks exercised no multi-item stockout ambiguity and no live
cross-unit reference-resolution example sufficient to assess the corresponding
new safeguards. Offline tests remain evidence of those mechanisms, not measured
race improvements. There is no observed AST, import or sandbox rejection in
these completed runs, and this analysis makes no new submission-compliance claim.

Concrete next-update sequence (proposed, not implemented)

1. Repair request-to-ledger coverage, with no search/interview rewrite.
   Preserve explicit use/occasion, required pattern/type, exclusions, and OR
   alternatives as distinct structured constraints. Tie constraints to the
   shopper's actual text; distinguish requirements from background anecdotes.
   Make an extraction omission or contradiction detectable before spending on
   catalog search. Reuse the existing extraction/review path where possible;
   do not add an unconditional expensive judge to every task.
   Review on recorded mules, logo, pants and tunik cases plus paraphrases and
   negative controls where the same words are merely background. A repair is
   not accepted if it invents new requirements or rejects either allowed OR arm.

2. Isolate the interview change in a separate checkpoint.
   Filter metadata unless explicitly requested, preserve meaningful planner
   priority, and avoid asking settled properties again. Treat catalog diversity
   as a limited tie-breaker, not the sole proxy for unknown shopper needs.
   Evaluate bounded multi-property coverage when messages are scarce and prior
   questions are unproductive, while retaining single questions when required
   or when the shopper rejects lists. Simply restoring unlimited old lists is
   not justified. Preserve the message reserved for required procedures.
   Replay actual watch and hoodie ranking inputs and use held-out/mutated
   catalogs with nuisance metadata. Never insert nylon, rectangle or girls as
   default answers: those values must come from the current shopper.

3. Reconcile candidate evidence and stockout recovery separately.
   Establish a consistent, item-scoped property interpretation shared by code
   checks and semantic review. Do not blindly prefer every scoped key: top type
   and Muslim top style can describe different predicates. Preserve raw keys
   and their evidence when resolving ambiguities and synonyms.
   Protect previously validated fitting/best candidates when constructing a
   bounded replacement context. Do not let recency remove the known minimum.
   Apply the same current requirements and ordering objective to replacements.
   Reserve enough actions for an observed cart mutation and another checkout
   before a late first purchase attempt. Exercise clock, skirt and pants
   traces, including explicit relaxations and alternatives that really fail.

4. Resolve the two public-evidence gaps before claiming complete remediation.
   Keep minimal reproductions for the failing long-sleeved top and first-run
   cleanser. Check their observed records and policy against any available
   public diagnostic clarification. If a future paid, narrowly selected probe
   is authorized, use it to discriminate competing explanations, not to rerun
   the full suite blindly. Do not reverse-engineer private grading state or
   hardcode the winning product from an older run.

5. Optimize spending after the accuracy paths are correct.
   Compact reviews around unresolved predicates and strongest candidates so
   the existing admission guard can afford them. Consider dependency-specific
   reuse of completed non-cart procedures. Verify 90-invocation reservations,
   missing billing, retry and fallback accounting again for any budget change.
   Do not relax the cap or mandate Sonnet everywhere based on these traces.

For every checkpoint, keep the previous source, capture the exact delta, replay
both the failure and passing counterexamples, and report which behavior changes
without pretending the replay yields a new benchmark score. Subsequent paid
validation, if requested, should first isolate the changed stage and then run
the fixed full task roster, recording all per-task gains and losses. A faster
70% run is not evidence that the accuracy target was met.

Review-process shortcoming

The earlier 50 offline checks verified execution and safety mechanics, but the
interview fixture effectively assumed that a varied catalog property was the
useful property. It did not challenge the ranker with real metadata, already
settled fields, or a relevant low-diversity property under a three-message cap.
The integrated scripted examples also did not establish extraction completeness
or consistent interpretation of generic versus scoped catalog keys. The build
report disclosed that limitation, but the review still left too much accuracy
risk in a combined candidate. These real traces now supply concrete regressions
that the next isolated reviews must cover. Passing more synthetic tests alone
will not demonstrate a score improvement.

Reproduction and evidence files

analyze.py reconciles all four main runs, checks source/world/request identity,
and saves only selected agent-visible trace fields. It excludes transport
headers, private grading records and model reasoning payloads.
analysis.json contains per-task scores, costs, times, calls, model/purpose
breakdowns and exact paired outcome lists.
tasks/<run>/<task>.json contains sanitized public observations, diagnostics and
visible model JSON/tool outputs, with request prompts for relevant review calls.
probe.py reproduces question overrides, the property conflict, lost predicates,
category rejection and the missing clock candidate with transport blocked.
probes.json and probe-output.txt record those results. They are diagnostic
replays, not new scored episodes.
verification.json records final source preservation and artifact checks.

Commands (offline):
  python3 logs/research/tosky-v4-local-review-2026-11-14/analyze.py
  python3 logs/research/tosky-v4-local-review-2026-11-14/probe.py

The workspace miner guide describes failure categories and explicitly notes
that a zero-scoring episode exposes a category rather than individual checks.
That evidence limit is why this report distinguishes reproduced defects from
supported causal explanations and unresolved evaluator disagreements.
