The requested sequence was followed with separate source checkpoints and

1. Requirement evidence and category alternatives

The previous category-specific-key rule could turn different semantic
dimensions into a false contradiction. V6 detects differing generic/scoped
evidence and returns unresolved instead of automatically selecting a winning
field or rejecting the product. The semantic reviewer receives the raw keys
and an explicit instruction to establish their meaning. A matching desired
value alone is not sufficient to choose a field.

Missing-property evidence no longer becomes a proven failure merely because
another listing carries that property. It is also not treated as proof of fit.
Automatic multi-item cart completion now uses the same positive-evidence
predicate as other automatic selections. References that have not been
resolved remain blocked.

An explicitly qualified property in an accepted audit quote retains its scope.
For example, replaying the actual audit output that shortened a property to
type now preserves type of top from its supporting quote. A matching title
word alone cannot establish an explicitly qualified property. This is generic
grammar handling, not a product synonym or attribute table.

Product alternatives remain valid through both fuller-label and named-label
cart checks. Ordinary explicit shelves and compound product kinds retain their
guards. A combined catalog label no longer invalidates an otherwise allowed
alternative.

Since ambiguous evidence is unresolved, recovery retains cheap unresolved
candidates in its bounded review context instead of excluding them before a
review. Retention does not authorize automatic purchase. The existing stricter
positive-proof condition remains for automatic cheapest substitutions.

Final review found and reproduced a preservation risk: four cheaper unresolved
candidates could displace an older proven replacement from this bounded context.
V6 now protects up to four proven replacements per affected item first, then
retains up to two unresolved candidates per item, followed by recent records.
The overall limit stays at 25. The failing counterexample now passes, and the
recorded cheaper clock is still present. This retains evidence for review; it
does not certify that either candidate is the correct final purchase.

Evidence:
- Five new falsifying tests fail on the starting source before these changes.
- Under the same recorded skirt observations, v5 rejects both relevant
  candidates; v6 marks the property ambiguous and keeps them eligible for
  review. This removes a false veto; it is not a newly measured task pass.
- The recorded Jeans candidate no longer receives the combined-category fault.
- The known cheap clock remains in the bounded recovery context. Its conflicting
  field spellings are now reviewed rather than mechanically declared proven.
- Exclusions, explicit property scope, numeric bounds, unrelated components,
  ordinary shelves and compound kinds have separate controls.
- The actual tunik extraction/audit response now yields the full qualified
  property. Whether a live review reaches the right final choice is unmeasured.

2. Review persistence and terminal recovery

Review keys now include the request, current ledger, budget, resolved references,
objective and relevant observed evidence. A rejected review remains rejected
when the same state is retried. New evidence or requirements cause a fresh
check. Interrupted, missing, malformed and internally contradictory replies
cannot silently become approval.

Final integration review also closed a fallback bypass: an unresolved product
needs an explicit successful semantic review before it can be ordered. The
controller does not equate absence of a deterministic rejection with proof.

Checkout is blocked for a known empty cart and known unavailable lines. The
same failed checkout is not repeated against unchanged evidence. The first
fallback provides a repair opportunity; if recovery still cannot establish a
valid order, the final fallback can use the existing decline tool and its
required procedure. A stale cart view no longer authorizes an empty purchase.

Evidence:
- The negative-review cache, interrupted review, changed evidence, changed
  budget and malformed-response contracts are tested separately.
- Replays of the two unfinished recorded states no longer repeat the empty
  or unavailable purchase. They reach a scripted acknowledged decline.
- That proves a legal control-flow ending, not a correct decline score; a task
  may still deserve an order when a fitting product has not been found.
- Required procedure proofs, their invalidation and scoped stockout handling
  retain their existing offline checks.

Artifacts: 02-before.txt, 02-after.txt, 02-public-replay.json,
checkpoints/02_review_finish.py.

3. Budget-aware recovery and context preservation

The per-invocation ceiling remains $0.050. The existing $0.004 allowance now
protects ordinary completion as well as stated procedures. It becomes available
when the main loop enters recovery, or when executing the required procedure.
This is progress-based behavior within an episode, with no phase, validator or
time-of-day routing.

The controller declines large structured-review requests whose budget would
reduce them to an unusably small output allowance. An empty length-limited
response is logged as unusable. Structured-response failures can receive one
priced fallback through the existing spare model; all physical attempts remain
charged, and credit/budget failures still stop calls.

Before an otherwise inadmissible planner request is refused, repeated
observation fragments can be replaced with references to a plain JSON table
containing the original data. Repeated narrative lines are handled similarly.
This transformation preserves chronological messages, tool-call/result pairs,
cache metadata, changed prices and stock, and every original observation value.
It does not select a smaller candidate pool or remove old evidence. Unique
histories that cannot shrink are returned unchanged. The original uncompressed
history remains in memory; references are generated solely from this episode's
observations. No encoded static answers, filesystem storage or new module is
used. The old method that clipped historical tool text has been removed.

Final review narrowed the trigger: an ordinarily admissible request is not
transformed merely because its output cap is somewhat reduced. Deduplication
is used when the request is inadmissible, or the previous history-size limit
would have caused truncation.

reference notation. That behavioral effect remains unmeasured and is a reason
to isolate this stage in any subsequent paid comparison.

The 90-invocation unknown-billing stress check includes both ordinary and
reserved recovery spending, and remains within the $4.50 configured allocation.
The corresponding 30-invocation allocation is $1.50. These calculations depend
on the configured model-rate envelope and invocation count; they are not a
guarantee against arbitrary provider-price changes or additional runs.

4. Questions with observable outcomes

Within an item, an unsuccessful question no longer causes the next message to
combine several properties. Each question requests one property. An ambiguous
answer to a legacy grouped question does not mark all unaddressed properties
as used or free. Explicit requested questions, answered-property exclusions,
metadata exclusion and procedure-message reservations remain in place.

The existing multi-item scheduling is retained. This change does not add a
fixed priority list of product attributes or change the planning model.

Evidence:
- A stricter scripted shopper responds only when its property is asked alone.
  The pre-change candidate misses that preference; the new candidate discovers
  it and completes the required explanation within the message allowance.
- Existing successful preference/procedure and stockout/procedure scenarios
  also remain successful in the preserved suite.
- This demonstrates a specific protocol improvement in the fixture. It does
  not establish ORO shopper behavior for every task or prove a higher discovery
  rate on the 30 real tasks.

Question priority remains a limitation. Atomic questions cannot guarantee that
every undisclosed property is reached with a three-message budget. The watch
and coat are not declared solved, and no task-specific priority was embedded.

Artifacts: 04-before.txt, 04-after.txt, checkpoints/04_atomic_questions.py.

5. Search-mode correction and progress feedback

When a keyword argument is present and a tool explicitly advertises one enum
value for keyword/text search, the controller selects that documented value.
It retains all other arguments. Pure filter searches and undocumented or
ambiguous mode choices are left unchanged. Both planner and internal calls use
the correction before contacting the environment.

Search progress is measured from returned listing data. Repeated IDs with new
prices, stock or other facts remain new evidence. Identical listing data triggers
a reminder that these results do not add candidate coverage, while new notes or
pagination may still matter. No search result is fabricated or used to assert
that the whole catalog has been exhausted.

The new progress feedback does not impose a blanket ban on refreshing results.
The inherited exact-call cache remains; this change does not expand it into a
cache across different keyword queries or different observed inventory states.

Evidence:
- Renamed tool and mode fixtures verify that choices come from the advertised
  contract rather than fixed public-world names.
- On the recorded pants and knit-clothing actions, the helper identifies 11 and
  three keyword-mode corrections, respectively.
- It identifies seven and three repeated listing-result sets in those traces.
- Actual search results for corrected queries have not been fetched. The knit
  task is not declared recovered merely because mode selection is corrected.

Artifacts: 05-before.txt, 05-after.txt, 05-public-search.json,
checkpoints/05_search.py.

6. Final verification and remaining limits

Final suite: 34 top-level unittest checks pass. One wrapper additionally runs
53 inherited checks against v6, including the six scripted success scenarios.
One former budget assertion that expected a severely shortened expensive-model
answer is intentionally replaced by an admission/recovery contract. Original
test files are unchanged. A test added during stage four was initially placed
under the wrong class; this fixture error was corrected before judging the
stage, and the retained pre-change result shows the three intended failures.

Final review added counterexamples for an unreviewed ambiguous fallback,
internally contradictory approvals, irrelevant component fields, qualified
property words in titles, recovery spending across 90 invocations, and uncertain
candidates displacing proven replacements. Their
checks and the preserved suite pass together. Public evidence, terminal-flow
and all-history preservation replays were rerun against the final source.

The source is 450,209 UTF-8 bytes with a synchronous agent_main. Local parsing
and compilation took about 0.26 seconds. Imports are unchanged; the local scan
finds no prohibited direct file/eval/exec calls or embedded task/product/world
IDs. The six new controller methods are reachable from the entry path. New
state is confined to the invocation. Only existing proxy environment and
inference paths are used. The new logic does not contain product synonym,
attribute-priority or unit registries.

or an agent established to win a race.
