Tosky_l17: repeated-question correction
Reviewed 2026-11-17. No paid inference or new environment evaluation was run.

Evidence and scope

The supplied local-9f2ac835e808440197a18a9a65313580 artifacts contain
30 completed practice episodes, 22 successful finishes, and 8 failed finishes.
The tested agent SHA256 is
929ab3a86af2b0669db18e9d44f13c8d6742db3ff3f2a55d985731684a0bb834,
which matches the source saved before this correction.

This is a practice delivery, not evidence about the hidden race score. The
failure labels are six request-not-met, one needs-not-found, and one
changes-missed. They do not disclose each exact sealed failed criterion.

There were 89 customer-reply interpretation calls: 84 recognized indifference
and 5 supplied firm requirements. All 55 actual follow-up questions immediately
after an indifferent interpretation repeated at least one exact topic from the
preceding question (377 repeated topic mentions). Thus repeated questioning is
a confirmed control-flow defect. Its frequency does not prove it caused most of
the eight failed scores: it also occurred in successful episodes.

Actual provider usage totals are 577 successful LLM calls, zero failed LLM
calls, and $0.34363575. The totals use one final usage record per problem, not
the cumulative inference_stats snapshots. Prompt tokens: 5,480,839; cached
prompt tokens: 4,261,160; completion tokens: 320,252. Median sandbox execution
time was 81.743 seconds, range 45.149 to 137.909 seconds. The episode-level
wall_seconds field repeats the whole-run time and is not per-agent latency.

Root cause

1. The model often already classified a reply as indifferent. The controller
   nevertheless settled a property only if the customer's raw reply literally
   contained all the property's stemmed words. An ordinary answer such as
   "Anything is fine" necessarily failed that test.
2. The next-question algorithm rotated the previous list in reverse order and
   added a few new properties. Unsettled topics therefore reappeared, commonly
   seven of the eight topics in a question.
3. The reply contract did not explicitly distinguish question-scoped, named,
   product-wide, and order-wide indifference. Generic answers could be mapped
   to a guessed first property or even a nonexistent property named "any".
4. Indifference lived only in the current interview object. Rebuilding that
   object could forget it. Newly disclosed requirements were also absent from
   the rebuilt object's initial stated requirements.
5. A single-topic question was marked done when sent, before knowing whether
   the customer had understood it. This is the opposite error: confusion could
   be counted as an answer.

Applicable guidance from the supplied book

AI_Agents_in_Depth_Bojie_Li_latest_en.pdf, Chapter 2:
- Section 2.4.3, printed page 62: organize a prompt as an ordered procedure.
- Section 2.4.4, printed pages 63-64: turn vague policies into executable
  decision rules rather than leaving business-policy choices to the model.
- Section 2.6, especially 2.6.2-2.6.4, printed pages 75-77: expose current task
  state near generation, retaining a stable system prefix.

These support an explicit answer-state transition and a computed status field.
They do not establish that any proposed shopping policy improves a benchmark.
Accordingly the correction changes both the reply instructions and their
controller consumer, with offline tests, rather than adding a prompt alone.

Implemented answer policy

- Unqualified indifference: settle all optional topics in the current question.
  Do not require the shopper to repeat the property names.
- Named indifference: settle only the named topic. Other topics remain open.
- Explicit product/order-wide opt-out: close optional preference interviewing
  in that scope; require supporting text quoted from the actual reply. Product
  scope uses uniquely matched supplied names, not a fuzzy cross-product guess.
- Firm answer: keep it as a requirement and stop asking about that topic.
- Mixed firm answer and indifference: preserve the firm value and settle only
  the remaining optional preferences in scope.
- Confusion, too many subjects, empty reply, or failed interpretation: do not
  reinterpret these as indifference. Keep the topic unresolved and use the
  existing narrower/reworded-question mechanism when allowed.
- A later explicit firm requirement overrides earlier optional flexibility.
- A general no-preference answer never erases the original firm requirements,
  budget, quantity, product category, or required approval procedure.
- Distinct parts remain distinct (for example body color versus handle color).
  Equivalent punctuation, camel case and simple plural spellings do not reopen
  the same topic. No product-specific synonym dictionary was introduced.

The existing initial question coverage is retained. In the recorded data,
useful disclosures occurred at the second or third topic of a list; changing
every message to a single topic would be an untested separate tradeoff.
After settled topics are removed, further questions may cover genuinely new
topics. The message limit is a ceiling, not a quota that must be exhausted.

Related mixed-reply safeguard

One recorded indifferent reply also lowered the customer's budget from 765 to
660 in the request's unit. The original reply contract excluded budgets from
product requirements but supplied no independent budget output, losing the
change. The new contract has independent budget and exact budget_quote fields.
The consumer validates a finite positive number and an actual source quote,
then updates the existing budget ledger and invalidates decision caches.
This is covered offline. The old saved model reply lacks those new fields, so
recorded-response replay cannot demonstrate new budget extraction. In that
episode the selected item cost 68, below either cap; the missed budget update
is not evidence for why that particular score was zero.

Verification performed in order

1. Saved the exact pre-correction source and matched it to the paid run.
2. Added fictional regression cases reproducing the state-transition defects.
3. Applied the changes only to interview state, its reply interpretation, the
   persistent item state and the computed current-status fields.
4. Ran the final 16 interview regression cases against the previous source:
   4 passed and 12 failed. No test errored.
5. Ran the updated source's full offline suite: all 64 tests passed, including
   all 16 interview cases and the prior 48 context, budget, transport and
   synthetic shopping-workflow checks.
6. Replayed all 89 saved reply interpretations independently through both
   controllers. For the 83 question-scoped indifferent cases, no settled topic
   or spelling alias appeared in the updated next question. All five firm
   disclosures produced exactly the same requirements before and after.
7. One saved mixed answer is treated as named indifference by the compatibility
   path because its old model output has no scope field and the raw reply names
   another property. Other topics remain open in this conservative case. The
   new prompt requests explicit scope; its model accuracy is not tested by this
   replay. This is not an 84/84 claim or a new full-run benchmark score.
8. Compared source ASTs: imports, budget constants, model choices, synchronous
   entrypoint, tool transport and schema-handling functions are unchanged.
   Compiled the source. Original Miyagi_v2 and Cairn_v2 archives retain their
   original hashes. No external ORO submission/AST service was invoked.

The reply prompt has more input state and output fields but uses the existing
single interpretation call, without a new LLM stage. Fewer repeated questions
can save messages, steps, latency and inference calls. Some freed messages may
be used for new topics, so a lower total cost is not guaranteed. The unchanged
per-task $0.05 admission allocation is $4.50 across 90 tasks; live provider
behavior and hidden-world token usage remain unmeasured by these offline tests.

Other failures observed, deliberately not bundled into this correction

The broader failed traces contain independent issues. Relevant evidence by
episode, with the limits of what is known:

- t-350bf2a022: conflicting material evidence between an unqualified field and
  a clothing-specific field; successive stock losses; remaining alternatives
  not exhausted. The visible evidence supports a review/evidence-precedence
  problem but does not reveal the grader's exact failed condition.
- t-559c052a99: the original request named a wedding occasion, but the brief
  extracted no firm requirements and the final item was casual. This omission
  is independent of the budget-update loss described above.
- t-86888552db: an initial category mismatch was repaired; later stock losses
  led to another choice with long-sleeve length and kimono sleeve style. The
  hidden criterion cannot be inferred conclusively from that conflict alone.
- t-9ab5c575f5: a request allowing pants OR jeans was repeatedly held by an
  internal check demanding a combined category. The agent described this as a
  store refusal even before sending checkout. The delay consumed opportunities
  to recover from a later stock loss. This is a separate category-OR bug.
- t-9fc67ee841: the brief shortened the expressly named Fall/Winter Clothing
  category to clothing and the agent selected another department. Category
  fidelity is a separate issue from asking.
- t-a6d85e34f3: three indifferent answers did not reveal the withheld need;
  later checkout feedback included a requirement revision. An actual hidden
  property cannot be invented from the failure label.
- t-abf1c45838: prolonged cheaper-item searching, internal inference admission
  refusals despite money remaining, and repeated checkout of the same cart
  after a stock error. A reasoning-setting change affecting conservative token
  accounting is a hypothesis requiring isolated reproduction before a fix.
- t-b516252b9f: repeated ineffective searches, incomplete pagination, and a
  decline assessment made before later listings were opened. A title suggesting
  a match is not conclusive proof of the sealed answer; audit freshness and
  evidence use need separate investigation.

These remain separate from the proven repeated-question mechanism. This patch
does not claim to resolve all eight scored failures or guarantee an improved
race score. A new paid verification is the user's next measurement step.
