4. Emergency finish bypasses rejection and recovery state

Relevant code: Cairn.finish and the ending path in run_calls.

The normal route checks offers, cart, facts and reviews. finish directly calls
checkout when it sees a held cart. It does not enforce all those preconditions.

In the bomber task, checkout at step 34 reports stock loss. The agent marks the
chosen listing sold out and has replacement candidates in its swap result.
Planning is then locally denied for the reason above. At step 35 forced finish
buys the SAME listing while sold_out is already true. The episode finishes and
receives changes-missed. This is a directly observed illegal state transition,
not a hypothesis about hidden requirements.

In the clock task, forced checkout at step 34 receives a nonterminal stock-loss
refusal. After further admission refusal, forced finish calls checkout again at
step 35 without repairing the cart. This also receives changes-missed. The
second retry need not be a network duplicate: it is a new step acting on an
unchanged cart after a known refusal.

Three fictional offline safety contracts fail against BOTH source versions:
- Repeating a review of an unchanged rejected cart must preserve the block.
- Emergency finish must not buy a cart with a current negative verdict.
- A stock refusal requires repair before another checkout of that cart.
All three fail by assertion; there are no diagnostic test execution errors.
These are inherited defects left unresolved by the last interview correction.

5. Review context is incomplete; rejection caching is also unsafe

Relevant code: Cairn.ledger, check_cart and take_note.

check_cart adds a key to self.checked before review. If the review says false,
the first attempt returns a warning. The next attempt for the same key returns
an empty string simply because the key is checked, even though self.verdicts
still contains false. The caller interprets the empty warning as permission to
continue. A checked flag is being used as if it meant passed.

The watch task demonstrates a second, CONNECTED problem. At search step 11 the
world returns a shopper update: "Never mind about color family, it was more of
a wish than a need." The NOTE interpreter correctly drops the original green
requirement. The final review receives the original request, which still says
green, and an active-only ledger, which no longer lists color. It does not
receive the explicit retirement and its source evidence. It rejects the
replacement watch for being rose gold rather than green. That objection is
stale: color had been explicitly withdrawn.

At checkout step 24 the replayed controller still has that negative verdict,
but sends checkout anyway. The MECHANICAL bypass is confirmed. However, the
color objection is not the cause established for this zero: the failure label
is needs-not-found, consistent with missing shape. Calling the rose-gold color
itself a violation would ignore the intervening customer change.

Consequently, simply making every negative review permanently binding could
make performance worse. Specialists need explicit current active AND retired
requirements, update evidence and scope. Then valid negative results should
persist until relevant evidence, requirements, quantities or cart contents
change. An evidence-backed appeal must remain possible.

9. Countermeasures, dependencies, and separate acceptance gates

Stage 1 — Repair mode-switch admission before claiming exhaustion.
  Component: wants_effort/call_model admission fallback, retaining existing
  quote_inference bounds, allocation, output caps and retry/time limits.
  If the optimization-mode request cannot be admitted, test the previously
  measured, unchanged mode before declaring the task exhausted. Do not remove
  token-count validation, assume cache discounts, or raise the cap.
  Acceptance: reproduce both quoted cases; admissible mode stays below the
  same cap; genuinely unaffordable requests still fail; unknown usage keeps its
  reservation; retries cannot overrun cost/time. No search/question changes.

Stage 2 — Make specialist task state explicit and consistent.
  Component: current-state construction used by CHECK and related specialists.
  Include active firm requirements, explicitly retired requirements, source
  updates and scope; distinguish unknown from waived. Preserve original text
  for evidence without making its superseded requirements authoritative.
  Acceptance: the withdrawn-color example is represented as retired; an
  unrelated firm need remains active; product A changes cannot mutate B;
  budgets, quantities, approval requirements and original values survive.
  Keep this state concise; avoid sending duplicated full histories to HEAR.

Stage 3 — Enforce validated decisions on every checkout path.
  Component: check_cart cache and finish/normal checkout preflight.
  A cached rejection remains a rejection until evidence or task/cart revision
  changes. All endings share checks for known stock loss, current requirements,
  relevant review state and mandatory procedures. A nonterminal stock refusal
  invalidates the prior candidate; a new step repeating the same cart is not
  recovery. Keep an evidence-backed path to re-evaluate an erroneous review.
  Stage 2 must precede strict rejection persistence: otherwise stale objections
  such as the withdrawn green constraint would become harder to correct.
  Acceptance: turn all three diagnostic contracts green; preserve positive
  checkout; repair to an available validated alternative; handle a legitimate
  review reversal; stop after finished:true; respect remaining step reserves.

Stage 4 — Replace ambiguous interview coverage with clear answer scope.
  Component: question selection/wording and HEAR settlement.
  Use one clear decision focus per message, with other listing values only as
  examples of that focus. A vague reply settles that focus, not a silent batch
  of independent properties. Explicit named or broader scope remains binding.
  Rank unresolved needs using both request clues and observed catalog evidence;
  keep the existing shopper-message ceiling and procedural-message reserve.
  Acceptance: scripted shoppers that answer one focus, explicitly waive many,
  change their mind, mix a budget update with a preference, or reject a crowded
  question. A hidden requirement in a previously mentioned but unanswered topic
  must remain discoverable; a confirmed indifferent topic must not repeat.
  Compare question budgets and preserve positive disclosure cases. These tests
  establish behavior, not a promised score gain from any ranking strategy.

Stage 5 — Repair original property/category bindings separately.
  Component: brief validation, exact-property evidence and OR-category checks.
  An explicit property/value relation must survive even when that value also
  appears in the product's name. OR must remain OR; a combined store label
  cannot silently replace permitted alternatives. Similar attribute names must
  not erase distinctions among properties or dimensions.
  Acceptance: fictional type-versus-style conflicts, part-specific colors,
  dimension conflicts, AND/OR category examples, explicit category restrictions,
  plain product names without an extra property, and successful existing cases.
  Do not use task IDs, fixed product/attribute tables or expected answers.

Each stage should have its own before/after review and offline regression
evidence. A paid evaluation after an isolated change is a measurement, not a
substitute for these diagnostic gates. No combination of these proposals can
yet be claimed to guarantee an improved race score.

The central engineering failure is promotion of partial information into
permission to proceed: an ambiguous answer becomes many waivers, a checked
cart becomes a passed cart, a held cart becomes safe for forced checkout, and
an omitted retired constraint becomes an active objection again. The concrete
repair is a shared, explicit task-state contract and an enforced checkout
boundary, with resource recovery kept available. Additional independent LLM
reviews alone do not restore information lost upstream.
